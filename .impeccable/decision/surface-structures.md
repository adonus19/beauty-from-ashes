# Surface structure candidates (ranked by resonance before each roll)

World is settled (DESIGN.md, Ash & Cobalt). These are compositions only.

## Get Help (/get-help), Persuade

1. **The path.** The five steps of asking become the page's spine: a brushed cobalt line runs down the page and each step carries the practical facts that belong to it (what to have ready under "Submit", privacy under "Review", cost under "Decision").
2. **Questions answered.** The page is the questions a hesitant applicant asks (Can I ask? What will it cost? What do I send? Who sees my photos? What happens next?), each a large serif question with a plain answer; a sticky question index on the left.
3. **The letter.** A centered letter from the ministry to someone unsure about asking, in the serif voice; the practical facts sit beside it as margin notes; the action closes the letter.
4. **Two doors.** The first viewport opens two paths, "For yourself" and "For your child", each with its own short copy and the same action; shared sections follow.
5. **Ready checklist.** Headline and reassurance at left, a numbered "what to have ready" checklist at right that ends in the Ask for help button; who, cost and privacy below.
6. **Intake reference.** A dense two-column reference with a sticky jump list (Who can ask, Cost, What to have ready, Privacy, Questions) and a sticky action.
7. **Promise band.** A centered promise band (free or reduced cost, open to everyone, private) like the mission band, then reference sections in asymmetric splits like the home page.

## Volunteer (/volunteer), Persuade

1. **Vision first.** The card's Vision statement is the first viewport's centerpiece (label, mission dash, large centered statement), with the page title and action beneath; the roles roster follows.
2. **A surgery day.** The page walks through one sample surgery day in time order and shows where each volunteer serves (arrival, prayer with the patient, the case, recovery, the drive home); labeled as a sample.
3. **The way in.** Joining as numbered steps (interest, conversation, credentialing, orientation, first surgery day), each step carrying the requirements that belong to it.
4. **Team board.** The first viewport is the surgical team as a roster board: each role on a line with what it involves and an "I can help" link.
5. **Two tracks.** Clinical and non-clinical volunteering side by side from the first viewport, each with its own roles and action.
6. **Clinician questions.** Licensure, liability, time, faith and location as large questions with plain answers.
7. **A colleague's letter.** A peer-to-peer letter from a volunteer clinician (labeled sample) with the roles in the margin.

## Give (/give), Persuade

1. **Form beside the case.** The first viewport splits: headline, lede and accountability line at left; the giving form (once or monthly, amounts, Give button) at right; then what gifts pay for, sponsor a surgery, other ways, accountability.
2. **One surgery, itemized.** The page is built around one surgery's costs as an itemized ledger (consultation, imaging, operating room, anesthesia, recovery) with placeholder figures; give toward one line or sponsor the whole surgery.
3. **Stewardship first.** Opens with the core-values belief about faithful stewardship, then accountability (501(c)(3), board, reports) before the form.
4. **Three ways.** Give once, give monthly and sponsor a surgery as three columns with one action each.
5. **A thank-you letter.** The page reads as the ministry's letter of thanks; the form sits in the margin.
6. **Every way to give.** A compact index of every way to give (card, check, donor-advised fund, stock, church offering, employer match, supplies) with online giving as the first entry.
7. **Photograph appeal.** The glaze photograph as a band with a centered appeal and the form below it.
